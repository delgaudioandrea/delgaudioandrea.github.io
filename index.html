The Lab

Tackling flood-related challenges and natural hazards by applying fundamental principles of fluid dynamics and mechanics

The GeoFlume Lab develops fully 3D eddy-resolving and 2D depth-integrated numerical models to predict geophysical and environmental fluid flows interacting with natural and human landscapes.

The Lab combines computational fluid dynamics, computational hydraulics and laboratory experiments to understand the flow physics and develop structural countermeasures and nature-based solutions to floods and natural hazards.

Research

Main research themes

01

Flood propagation and mitigation of geological hazards associated with floods and dam break events

Development of numerical tools and experimental analysis for flood propagation and dam break flows of Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids with a focus on mud flows using fully-3D, non-hydrostatic large eddy simulation (LES) models with deformable free surface capabilities

02

Eco-hydraulics and Shallow flows

Investigation of flow hydrodynamics in vegetated channels using large eddy simulations with a focus on characterizing flow structure inside and downstream of vegetation patches and drag forces acting on the plant stems.

03

Pump intake flows

Develop model and perform state-of-the-art eddy resolving numerical simulations to predict flow and vortical structures in pump bays. Development of numerical tools to assess design of complex multi-bay pump stations and effectiveness of vortex suppressing devices.

04

Morpho-dynamics

Characterization of active channels in ephemeral rivers using DinSar satellite data. I developed a new procedure for determining sediment transport and morphodynamical changes after floods events.
